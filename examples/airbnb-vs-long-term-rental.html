Airbnb vs. long-term rental: a worked example where the management fee decides

A short-term rental brings in more revenue, but it also costs more to run. Whether the extra revenue survives comes down mostly to who does the work. Here is one example duplex run both ways, month by month.

DoorMath · Published September 27, 2026 · All figures are for a made-up example property, not a real listing or market data.

The setup

The same example duplex and the same loan both ways: $239,000, 25% down, 7.00% for 30 years, so the mortgage is $1,192.55 a month either way.

Long-term: both units rent for $2,500 a month plus $60 of other income, with 6% vacancy, 5% maintenance and 5% CapEx, $200 a month of utilities and other costs, and a property manager at 8% of collected rent. These are the free calculator's example inputs.

Airbnb: the whole duplex is rented by the night.

  • Nightly rate and occupancy change by month (table below); stays average 3 nights.
  • Cleaning: guests pay a $95 cleaning fee per stay, and the cleaner costs $85 per turnover.
  • Platform fee 3% of revenue; supplies $8 per booked night.
  • Utilities $380 a month and other costs $120 a month, all paid by the owner; insurance $2,200 a year; the same $3,300 of property taxes.
  • Maintenance and CapEx 5% + 5% of revenue.
  • Furnishing $16,000, paid up front.
  • Management: a co-host at 20% of revenue.

The nightly rates and occupancy below are example inputs, not data for any real market. Use rates and occupancy from comparable listings near the property.

The Airbnb brings in $23,148 more revenue but makes $1,785 less cash flow. The co-host's 20% alone is $10,405 a year. It also needs $16,000 more cash up front for furnishing.

It breaks even at 65.7% occupancy, and the example projects 66.3%: a cushion of about 2 booked nights a year. A slightly slow year would wipe out the profit.

Now the Airbnb earns $6,310 more a year, and it breaks even at 47.0% occupancy, far below the 66.3% projected. That extra money is also your pay for the work: guest messages, turnovers, pricing, restocking and repairs between stays.

Before you decide

  • Local rules. Many cities license, restrict or ban short-term rentals, and many add taxes on each stay. Check before you buy.
  • Your own market data. Nightly rates and occupancy vary widely by market and season. Use figures from comparable listings near the property.
  • Loan and insurance. Check that your mortgage and insurance policy allow short-term rental use.
